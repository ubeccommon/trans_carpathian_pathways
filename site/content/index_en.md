::: {#vision .block}
## Vision

Carpathian nature and Carpathian culture are one inheritance, made and kept by people living with the land. We see mountains that stay alive because people live in them: where the meadows are mown and the crafts are in use, and where newcomers learn the Hutsul way of life beside those who kept it.

Over time, the places hosts choose to share and the paths between them will trace corridors where cultural, natural and landscape heritage are cared for as one whole — not wilderness, but a lived-in landscape whose people can say what their land is worth before others decide it for them.
:::

::: {#mission .block}
## Mission

Trans-Carpathian Pathways connects the people who still keep Carpathian mountain life alive with the Pilgrims of the Plai: people who want to meet them, slowly and on foot. It builds three things: a network of hosts who offer what they actually have — a bed, a meal, guidance, a craft — from where they live; a pathway that grows from where those hosts are rather than being drawn above them; and the people who keep it — pathway stewards who care for each stretch of the path, and local Curators who hold the whole and suggest, never sell, a journey to each pilgrim. There is no booking calendar, and each host decides what is shown about their place.

The *plai* is the old way through the high pastures, along which the flocks were driven up the mountain and down; the map the project runs on takes its name from it. Whoever comes on foot through the network is a Pilgrim of the Plai — in Ukrainian *прочанин плаю* — on the way to the people and the land that keep it, not to a shrine.

We begin in the Hutsul highlands of western Ukraine, between Vorokhta, Verkhovyna and Kosiv, and mean to cross every border on the arc, with a partner initiative in each country opening its own corridor.
:::

::: test
Regeneration, not mitigation. The measure is not how many pilgrims came, but one question: **is this place more alive, and more able to go on, than it was before the pathway?**
:::

::: {#stage .block}
## Where we are

**Built.** Plai, the map, carries a host's place with what they offer, when, for how many, in which languages and how it is reached; the host places the point and decides what is shown, and the Curator publishes. Since October 2026 anyone can plan a way on it — "Plan a way", from a start to an end, footpaths first, roads only where nothing else joins — and a steward or Curator can keep a way as a stretch and put stretches together as a corridor; only the Curator publishes. The project's documents are here, in English and Ukrainian with German and Polish translations, and its record is public.

**Not yet.** No host and no pathway steward is named; the first Curator is the project lead for now, until a core body is formed. The co-creator agreement is a draft. The first corridor has not been walked. The first hosts come only after off-host backups are in place and the data-protection read is done, as the agreement requires.

**The test of the concept** — a host, a pilgrim, the honest offer and the contribution, tried on a short stretch of the corridor — runs with this review and changes as it learns.

We are looking for hosts, stewards and Curators in the corridor, partners along the arc, contributors, and funders for the pilot.
:::

## Read, and answer

Four drafts are out for public review: the [overview](overview/), the [proposal](proposal/), the [white paper](whitepaper/) and the [co-creator agreement](agreement/). Each is a page here with its PDF, in English and Ukrainian; the overview, the proposal and the white paper are also in German and Polish, in translations that no speaker of those languages has read yet. The Ukrainian texts have not yet been read by people in the corridor, and that reading is asked of them before the review travels further.

Answer by mail to [stewardship@ubec.network](mailto:stewardship@ubec.network), or open an issue on [the project's record on GitHub](https://github.com/ubeccommon/trans_carpathian_pathways/issues). There is no closing date: the review runs as long as the drafts are drafts, and every decision it leads to is written in the record with its date.

::: {#direct .block}
## If you come directly

A pilgrim may find a published host on the map and go without asking anyone. Then you prepare yourself: read your own government's travel advice, know that Ukraine is under martial law, carry what insurance you can obtain, and inform yourself about cash and about permits. What the project knows of the law it publishes here; it is not legal advice.

- Ivano-Frankivsk oblast's curfew runs from midnight to five in the morning.
- During an air-raid alert everyone follows the official warning and goes to the nearest shelter the host knows of; a journey pauses.
- Along the state border with Romania runs a strip five kilometres wide. Entering it — leaving a village into forest or polonyna — needs written permission from the border detachment, decided within ten days and issued as a QR code; a village itself needs none. The Verkhovyna district is a controlled border district, and several routes towards the Chornohora ridge are closed or restricted. How a foreigner obtains that permit in practice is not yet established, so the project suggests no stage inside the strip to anyone.

Stated as the [white paper's §10.2](whitepaper/#s10-2) states it, from the State Border Guard Service's announcement of December 2023, reporting of July 2024 and October 2025, and the Verkhovyna National Nature Park, read on October 9, 2026. Nothing here replaces your own enquiry or, if you come through the Curator, the Curator's briefing.
:::

::: {#recognise .block}
## How to recognise the project

The project writes only from [stewardship@ubec.network](mailto:stewardship@ubec.network); anyone else who claims to speak for it is not the project. Nobody from it — the lead, a Curator or a steward — asks for money in advance, a deposit, a password, or a passport or a copy of it. A pilgrim pays the host, as they agreed; the project holds no money for stays and takes no share.
:::
