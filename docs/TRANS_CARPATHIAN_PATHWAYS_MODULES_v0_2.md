*Michel's question, October 9, 2026: should the project be seen as
one comprehensive whole made of building blocks and the blocks that
connect them — cultural and natural heritage; technical
infrastructure (GIS, data security and protection, standards, a
multilingual platform); spirituality, regeneration and
relationship? Read against prompt v0.33, the white paper v0.3, the
reasoning v0.1, proposal v0.3, agreement v0.3, the stress tests and
the partners long list v0.2.*

* * *

## 1. The answer

**Yes — as a map, not as a plan to build everything.** Seeing the
project whole, in modules, does three things the documents do not
yet do. It shows a partner where they fit: a heritage funder funds
heritage, a university takes the record, a GIS developer takes
Plai, and nobody has to fund or understand the whole. It lets each
module carry its own gate, its own holder and its own measure, so
work can start in one without waiting on all. And it puts the
project's commitments where they belong — in the connections
between modules, as the standards each connection keeps — so that a
partner who takes one module cannot quietly change what binds the
others.

The idea is not new in these mountains. The Ukrainian regional
studies and tourism society *Plai*, founded in Lviv in 1924, joined
in one body the study of the land, the protection of natural and
archaeological monuments, the marking of trails, the building of
shelters and publishing; it built what is described as the first
Ukrainian mountain shelter for tourists, on the Plisce meadow in
the Gorgany, in 1933–35 [1]. It is a precedent for the shape, and
for the name.

**The condition: modules are named before they are opened.** The
project's own rule is work to need: a capability is built when the
work requires it, not before. A comprehensive map read as a plan
turns a corridor of three to five hosts into an institution with
twelve departments and one person — the founder scenario of the
stress tests, and the Via Dinarica lesson of the white paper (a
programme designed before its steward). So every module on the map
has one of three states: **active** (a gate needs it and someone
holds it), **named** (defined, waiting for need and a holder), or
**later**.

## 2. The map

**The spine — relationship, spirituality, regeneration.** Not a
module. It is what every module is measured by: the principles,
the reasoning, and the regenerative test — is the place more alive
and more able to go on than before the pathway. Made a module, it
becomes a deliverable someone is paid to produce, which is how the
words are borrowed cheaply (white paper §5.3). Kept as the spine,
it is the question each module answers at its gate.

## 3. The connectors — where the standards live

A connector is the promise one module makes to another. Each names
the standard it keeps. **Decided** means on Michel's dated word or
built. All of the table's proposals were decided on his word, October 9,
2026 (§6).

## 4. What goes wrong with a comprehensive project, and the rule for each

1. **It becomes the institution it said it would not be.** *Rule:* a
   module opens only when a gate needs it and someone holds it;
   until then it is named, with its state, and nothing more.
2. **One person holds twelve modules.** *Rule:* every active module
   names who holds it. A module only Michel could hold waits, unless
   the current gate cannot pass without it. Today that is P1, P3,
   T1, T2 and G1 — which is the stress tests' finding in another
   form.
3. **A partner takes one module and bends it.** A funder pays for
   T1 and asks for an app; a tourism body takes H1 and makes a
   catalogue. *Rule:* the connectors in §3 bind whoever holds a
   module, and the P block — people on the ground — is funded
   before any infrastructure module is grown.
4. **Heritage spoken for.** H1 and H2 could describe a village's
   culture and land in the project's words. *Rule:* they record
   only what hosts chose to share, with the consent of §3; a
   corridor speaks for its points, never for a place.
5. **The spine turned into a product.** *Rule:* nobody is funded to
   produce spirituality or relationship; they are what each gate is
   asked about.

## 5. What the pilot pays for, and what partners bring

The recommended €25,700 covers the P block, T2's legal reads and
backups, a little of T1, T3 and T4, and G1's forming of the body —
the proposal's funding table, unchanged. The H block and G3 have no
line, and need none from the pilot: they are where partners bring
their own money and skill — a heritage funder (F-6, F-7) for H1, a
park and a grassland team (I-7, SR-4, SR-5) for H2, a university for
G3 (SR-1 to SR-3). Read this way, the long list is not one search
for one pilot's money but a set of doors, one or two per module.

## 6. Decided — Michel's word, October 9, 2026

All six points, as proposed:

1. **The map.** Four blocks and twelve modules, as §2 sets them out,
   are how the project describes itself.
2. **What is active now.** For the test stretch and Gate 3: P1, P2,
   P3, T1, T2 and G1; T3 and T4, already under way. Named, waiting:
   H1, H2, G2, G3. A named module opens on a gate's need and a holder,
   on his word.
3. **The spine.** Relationship, spirituality and regeneration stay the
   spine, not a module; the regenerative test is asked at every gate.
4. **Marking.** Whether the pathway is marked, and to which standard,
   is settled at Gate 4, after the corridor is walked.
5. **Standards.** A BCP 47 language tag in every document, page, PDF
   and diagram; WCAG 2.2 AA for the site, checked by its build; ISO
   19115 metadata with an INSPIRE-compatible profile for a layer
   published for others to use, and only then; CARE for the people in
   any study, FAIR for the data that may be open.
6. **The name.** The society *Plai* of 1924 is named in the white
   paper (v0.4, §6.2) as the precedent for Plai's name.

What was done the same day: the white paper v0.4 in both languages and
its PDF; the language tags in the four PDF builds and the two diagram
builds, all rebuilt; WCAG 2.2 AA in the site's stylesheet (v0.4) and
build (v0.4), with what only a person can check in `site/README.md`.
