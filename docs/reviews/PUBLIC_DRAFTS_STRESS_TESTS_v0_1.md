*Michel's word, October 10, 2026: review the documents and do two
or three stress tests, update accordingly. The documents were the
six proposed that evening for public review: the white paper v0.7,
the proposal v0.5, the co-creator agreement v0.5 (each in English
and Ukrainian), the project overview v0.3, the reasoning v0.2 and
the open-data specification v0.2. Three tests were run, each by a
separate agent that had not written the documents, each read-only,
each told to quote, to label general knowledge as such and to
avoid repeating the stress tests of October 9: the corridor reader
(a Hutsul grandmother reading only Ukrainian, an official of
Kosiv's Department of Culture and Tourism, a young family of
newcomers), the hostile reader (an attacker, someone with bad
intent in wartime, a lawyer or journalist looking for a quote, a
scammer), and the fact-checker a careful funder would hire. They
made 75 findings. Each report's verdict is given in §2 to §4 as
delivered, with a line for every finding and what was done with
it. "Taken in" means corrected in the documents named, in both
languages where both exist. "Decision" means it is a choice, not a
correction, and is listed in §5 for Michel's word. Two load-bearing
claims were checked again on the web before acting: the Svydovets
figures against Bruno Manser Fonds' own page (230 km of pistes, 60
hotels, 390 holiday homes; no lift count) and Fairbnb's liquidation
against Co-op News of April 7, 2025 (supported; kept).*

* * *

## 1. What the three found in common

- **The drafts were behind the decisions.** The white paper, the
  reasoning, the open-data specification and parts of the agreement
  still called open what Michel had decided on October 9: how
  payment passes, the briefing of pilgrims from abroad, how a
  Curator is chosen, carrying capacity, the meeting-place point,
  corridor consent, short logs, the `geo:` link. All three
  readers would have quoted the contradiction.
- **"Open record" was not true.** The repository is private. Every
  document now describes the record as it is; whether to publish
  it, or a scrubbed mirror, is Michel's (§5, D-3).
- **One person is the single point of every promise**, and the
  texts said where he lives, how a password is reset and what
  backups are missing. The texts now state the dependence plainly
  without the operational detail, and add how to recognise the
  project.
- **The Ukrainian has not been read in the corridor.** The paper
  says so itself; the corridor reader found the places where a
  local would read the wrong meaning. Those are corrected; the
  reading itself is §5, D-2.
- **Claims about named third parties** (Bukovel's owners and a
  forgery case, NGO figures stated as the project's own, a
  partner candidate's visitor counts) needed attribution, and got
  it.

## 2. The corridor reader

readable, but in many places it reads as a translation, and it
fails worst at phrases a local reads literally and wrongly —
«сума залишається в Куратора», «громаді, якій належить Буковель»,
«публічне право», «громадська валюта» and «Перехід» used as a
project gate. The larger problem is voice: every document is
signed by UBEC, the only contact is Michel's email, the Ukrainian
has not been read by anyone in the corridor, and several lines
speak about locals rather than to them — hosts "play hospitality",
a newcomer "matters most", councils asked for residents' names.
None of it is fatal; most is fixable in text; C-1 to C-8 should be
settled before public review.

## 3. The hostile reader

*Verdict, as delivered:* The design is honest; publishing the
drafts as they stand would hand over too much. The worst exposures
are not where homes are but how the service works around its one
person — who holds every key, where he lives, how a password is
reset, what backups are missing. Three promises the texts cannot
keep: an "open repository" that is private, border rules "told to
every pilgrim" when pilgrims may come directly, and legal reads
"before the first pilgrim" while the overview says a pilgrim test
is under way. A hostile lawyer or journalist would also find
Bukovel linked to forged land acts by juxtaposition, a
tourism-law self-assessment that reads as a plan to avoid
licensing, an exchange of work offered to foreigners that the
project's own paper calls legally unsettled, and a published
placeholder where the reason for showing homes at their exact
point should be. Nearly everything can stay honest with narrower
wording.

## 4. The fact-checker

*Verdict, as delivered:* The drafts should not go out for public
review as they are. The numbers mostly hold — the budget adds up,
the tourist-tax ×4.4, the 6.5:1 house-price gradient, the 17,000
displaced and the distances check out, and most web-checked claims
are accurate. The serious problem is staleness: decisions of
October 9 were not carried into the white paper (v0.4 to v0.7),
the reasoning, the open-data specification or parts of the
agreement, so the white paper tells outside readers that payment,
the briefing, the Curator's selection and the budget are open, and
its §11 is the morning of October 9. Three more would embarrass
the project: every document says the record is open while the
repository is private; the agreement's principles are not "the
same list" as the proposal's; and the proposal lists the host
fields as "to be built".

## 5. Decisions for Michel

Before the drafts go out:

- **D-1 — The Ukrainian name.** «Транскарпатські стежки» reads as
  Zakarpattia oblast. Keep it, or use «Карпатські стежки» or
  «Стежки через Карпати» in Ukrainian.
- **D-2 — A reading in the corridor.** Vasyl Yuskiv and two
  residents read the Ukrainian texts before publication; the cover
  byline «UBEC і «Жива хата»»; a Ukrainian-speaking contact; one
  sentence on what Zhyva Khata is.
- **D-3 — The record.** Keep the repository private (the texts now
  say so) or publish a mirror of the outward documents. If the
  whole repository is ever made public, the reviews, the README,
  the prompt and the plan are scrubbed first. *Taken, October 11,
  2026: the record is public. Checked before the texts said so: no
  address, password, token or key anywhere in the history; what
  remains is operational detail — the host's name and users, the
  paths, the backup plan, the October 9 scenarios.*
- **D-4 — The test of the concept.** Say what it is — off the
  platform, among people the project already knows, no pilgrim
  from abroad — or take it out of the overview. *Taken, October
  11, 2026: the test runs with the public review and changes as
  it learns.*
- **D-5 — The first Curator.** "No Curator by name" against S-9's
  "the first Curator is Michel": one wording for the white paper,
  the overview and the plan.
- **D-6 — The backups line.** Whether public texts say that
  off-host backups do not exist yet, or only that they are the
  condition of the first real account.
- **D-7 — No ratings.** Confirm it as decided, or mark it as
  proposed in the proposal and the overview too. *Taken, October
  11, 2026: no ratings unless those rated consent. The agreement
  carries it, and the public record, at its next version; v0.7,
  on Plai, stays true meanwhile.*
- **D-8 — The agreement on Plai.** Plai records v0.3. Ask for a
  Plai migration linking the current version before review, or
  keep the note now in the agreement. *Taken, October 11, 2026:
  agreement draft v0.7 on Plai (mapservice `plai/007`).*

When they bear on the next version:

- **D-9** — An exchange of work not offered to pilgrims from abroad
  until the Ukrainian legal read.
- **D-10** — ubec-pay: who keeps the mutual-credit accounts, under
  which law; or the line that neither UBEC nor the project does.
- **D-11** — The journey file: named waypoints, or numbered with
  names sent apart.
- **D-12** — Families with small children while the war lasts.
- **D-13** — A line on how the documents were drafted, in place of
  the authorship notes.
- **D-14** — The Hungary detail and the President's meeting in
  white paper §1.3.
- **D-15** — Whether a named deputy is a condition of the first
  real host.
- **D-16** — A pilgrim sends passport data for a border permit to
  the border guard themselves, never through the Curator.
- **D-17** — The reason for showing homes at their exact point,
  in Michel's words, before the reasoning is published.
- **D-18** — A one-page note for hosts; signing at the second visit.
- **D-19** — Whether Gate 3 waits for a Ukrainian entry form.
- **D-20** — What the hromada gets: a report at each gate.
- **D-21** — T-3: that the project borrows no one's faith, and
  chapels never marked without the parish.
- **D-22** — «Куратор», and lowercase in Ukrainian.
- **D-23** — «Перехід» or «поріг» for a gate.
- **D-24** — Newcomers host on the same terms.
- **D-25** — Stewards for twelve months or eighteen.

Also open, not new: S-14's council step; which documents go on

documents published for review are on the project's site, which
