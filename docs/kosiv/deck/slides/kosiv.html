Pilgrims carried to the people who make things

Workshops as encounters

A potter, weaver or carver offers a workshop from where they work. The pilgrim learns by doing; the maker gains a reason to keep the craft.

Income that stays

Meals and workshops carry as much village income as beds. Payment goes from pilgrim to host; the project takes no share.

A living cultural route

One concrete form of the shared cultural routes the Carpathian Eight declaration calls for, built from the people outward.

Newcomers and those who stayed

Families who come to learn the Hutsul way of life find the people who hold it, on the same honest terms.

Trans-Carpathian Pathways · 08

Kosiv is the corridor's craft end. Research on village tourism, for example the Overseas Development Institute's work, finds that the food supply chain and workshops carry as much of poor people's tourism income as the bed. Over time, the places hosts choose to share can also show where culture, landscape and nature still hold together: a map a community can use when decisions about its land are made. We are careful here: a corridor speaks only for the hosts who chose to be on it, never for what a community wants. Only the community can say that.
