# Trans-Carpathian Pathways

*A living network of hosts, paths and Curators in the Hutsul highlands — project handout for the Department of Culture and Tourism, Kosiv*

October 10, 2026 · Michel Garand

## In brief

Trans-Carpathian Pathways is a network of local hosts who share what they actually have — a bed, a meal, guidance or a craft — from where they live. A pathway emerges from where those hosts are, and local Curators and pathway stewards hold each stretch of it.

The first corridor runs from **Vorokhta through Verkhovyna to Kosiv** in the Hutsul highlands. The project is initiated by **UBEC** (Ubuntu Bioregional Economic Commons), led by Michel Garand, and **Zhyva Khata**, led by Vasyl Yuskiv.

It runs on **Plai**, an open-source, consent-controlled map of the whole Carpathian range that UBEC already operates on its own server in the European Union. There is no booking platform, no rating and no commission: payment is the host's to set, and every host owns their own entry.

## Why now

The Carpathians are being re-valued from outside faster than from within, while the people who hold the Hutsul way of life grow older.

- **Property.** An average house was listed at about 1.9 million hryvnia in Kosiv and 1.8 million in Verkhovyna in October 2025, against 11.7 million in Polianytsia, the hromada of Bukovel ([Zaxid.net](https://zaxid.net/budinok_v_karpatah_skilki_koshtuye_i_vid_chogo_zalezhit_tsina_n1620372)). The gap is what makes the corridor attractive to buyers.
- **Visitors.** Oblast tourist-tax receipts rose from 10.4 million hryvnia in 2021 to 46.2 million in 2025 ([oblast tourism department](https://speakerdeck.com/iftourism/turizm-ivano-frankivskoyi-oblasti2025)).
- **The land.** Cattle in private households of Ivano-Frankivsk oblast fell from 109,500 in 2021 to 76,000 in 2024 ([Suspilne](https://suspilne.media/ivano-frankivsk/968067-ak-na-frankivsini-zmensuetsa-ciselnist-koriv-statistika-z-1991-roku/)). The polonynas were made by mowing and grazing and degrade when that stops.
- **Policy.** The Carpathian Eight declaration of September 2026 commits to cultural landscapes, the traditional way of life of mountain communities and shared cultural and tourist routes ([declaration](https://www.president.gov.ua/en/news/deklaraciya-samitu-karpatskoyi-integracijnoyi-iniciativi-106445)). Of its 90 listed projects, worth $10.7 billion, energy accounts for $7.28 billion ([Kyiv Post](https://www.kyivpost.com/post/84863)). Culture has a sentence; it does not yet have an instrument.

The mountains are also being re-inhabited. Kosiv holds more than a hundred craft workshops ([ReHERIT](https://reherit.org.ua/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Kosiv_profil-spadshhyny_REHERIT.pdf)); Kosiv painted ceramics are on UNESCO's intangible heritage list since 2019 ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/tradition-of-kosiv-painted-ceramics-01456)); and 5,702 displaced people were registered in the Kosiv hromada by March 2025 ([Suspilne](https://suspilne.media/ivano-frankivsk/977307-u-akih-gromadah-ivano-frankivsini-zivut-najbilse-pereselenciv/)). Pathways stands with this second movement.

## How it works

Three things interlock: hosts who offer what is real, a pathway that emerges between them, and the people who hold it.

| Role | What they do |
| --- | --- |
| Host | Offers one or more of an overnight stay, a meal, local guidance or a workshop, described as it is. Sets their own months, capacity, languages and how the place is reached. |
| Pathway steward | Cares for a stretch of path, knows its hosts and places, may enter a place for a host who cannot. |
| Curator | A person of the corridor — often a farmer, teacher or walker — who reads every place before it is published, holds the safety knowledge, and suggests (never sells) a journey to each pilgrim. |
| Pilgrim of the Plai | Walks a stretch of the plai — the old path through the high pastures — for an afternoon or a month, to learn rather than to look, and arrives as a guest. |

**The pathway is not drawn from above.** Each host becomes a point; the paths between them follow existing trails; the Curator adjusts for terrain, season and capacity. Springs, viewpoints and other places worth arriving at are mapped along the way.

**Plai already exists.** It holds a base map of the whole Carpathian range, an entry surface where a person places a point exactly where they mean it, consent field by field, withdrawal from every map and export, and the host fields — built and tested in October 2026. The name echoes *plai*, the mountain path from pasture to pasture, and the Lviv regional-studies society *Plai* of 1924.

## Regeneration, not only sustainability

Sustainable tourism asks how an activity can continue with less harm. Regeneration asks what a place needs to become more itself. In mountains whose meadows degrade when left alone, less harm is not enough.

|  | Sustainable development | Regeneration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The question | How can this continue with less harm? | What does this place need to become more itself? |
| The people | A stakeholder group whose needs are addressed | Participants in the place's life, and the ones who know it |
| The place | A site where generic standards apply | The source of the design, each one different |
| The aim | Neutral: no net harm | Net positive: more alive than before |

The project's own test is one question, asked at every stage: is this place more alive, and more able to go on, than it was before the pathway?

## What it can mean for Kosiv

Kosiv is the corridor's craft end, and the pathway is built to carry pilgrims to people who make things, not past them.

- **Workshops as encounters.** A potter, weaver or carver can offer a workshop from where they work. The pilgrim learns by doing beside someone who knows; the maker gains a reason, and a means, to keep the craft.
- **Income that stays in the village.** Research on village tourism finds meals and workshops carry as much local income as lodging ([ODI](https://cdn.odi.org/media/documents/110_yFKzu3c.pdf)). Payment goes from pilgrim to host, in forms the host chooses; the project takes no share.
- **A living route, not a display.** Pathways is one concrete form of the shared cultural routes the Carpathian Eight declaration calls for, built from the people outward.
- **Newcomers and those who stayed.** Families arriving to learn the Hutsul way of life can find the people who hold it, on the same honest terms.
- **Evidence the community owns.** Over time the places hosts share can show where culture, landscape and nature still hold together — a map a community can use when decisions about its land are made.

## What the project will not do

Each refusal guards against the ways community tourism has failed elsewhere.

- **No booking calendar.** Availability is a conversation with the Curator; a calendar turns a host into a supplier with slots to fill.
- **No ratings.** A score teaches a host to perform for the score.
- **No commission.** The project, its Curators and stewards take no share of what a pilgrim pays a host.
- **No staged tradition.** The encounter is real or it is nothing.
- **No one speaks for a community.** A host speaks for their own place, a Curator for what they publish, the project for itself.
- **No data sold, lent or tracked.** Each person decides what is shown, field by field, and can withdraw at any time.

## Where the project stands

The map and the documents exist; the hosts do not yet. Each gate opens when its work is done, not on a date.

| Stage | State | What it holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gates 0 to 2: decisions and the map | Done | Decisions recorded; host fields and consent built and tested on Plai (October 2026) |
| A test of the concept | Under way | A host, a pilgrim, the honest offer and the contribution, tried on a short stretch of the corridor |
| Gate 3: the first hosts | Next | Three to five hosts entered with the Curator beside them; opens after off-host backups and the data-protection read |
| Gate 4: the corridor walked | Then | Vorokhta to Kosiv end to end, once per season, for two seasons; distance, time and what a pilgrim needed recorded per stage |
| Phase 2: the corridor decides | Later | The corridor's hosts, stewards and Curators choose how the project is governed, and which corridor comes next |

**In place.** The proposal, white paper, co-creator agreement and user manual in English and Ukrainian; Plai with the host fields and consent built in. A test of the concept is under way on a short stretch of the corridor.

**Still to come.** Off-host backups before the first real account; legal reads on data protection and on Ukrainian tourism and tax rules before the first pilgrim; a legal body for the project; funding for the pilot.

## What we ask of the Department

We ask for attention and counsel, not endorsement.

1. **A reading.** Your view of the project, and the questions the department — and the villages it knows — would want answered before anyone appears on a map.
2. **Introductions.** To craft centres, museums, the Institute of Applied and Decorative Arts and people who already host, teach a craft, herd or mow. Each person decides for themselves whether to join; no one is chosen for them.
3. **Local guidance.** How home hosting, meals and the tourist tax work in practice in the Kosiv hromada, so Curators can brief hosts accurately.
4. **A place in the conversation.** Consideration of Pathways as a pilot within the community's cultural-route planning, and in its contact with the Carpathian Eight and the Carpathian Convention, whose presidency Ukraine takes after the conference at the end of 2026.
