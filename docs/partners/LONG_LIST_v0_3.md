## 1. The project, as a partner would read it

**What a partner will find strong.** The thinking is unusually
honest: the white paper names its own failure modes with sources,
refuses to count success in pilgrims, and keeps open items open.
The map is not a promise: Plai exists, with consent as columns,
withdrawal, export and a leak test, so a funder's money goes mostly
to people rather than to software. The design already carries the
lessons comparable projects paid for — a named steward per stretch,
no booking layer, payment direct from pilgrim to host. And its
frame — the meadows made and kept by mowing and grazing, heritage
and nature as one — is the frame the grassland science, IUCN
Category V and UNESCO's continuing landscape already use.

**What a partner will ask about first.**

1. **Who receives the money.** Of the funders researched (§3 and
   the list in §5), nearly every grant-maker needs a registered
   grantee. The fiscal hosts do not; one small funder may accept an
   informal initiative (F-5, unverified); one accepts Canadian
   individuals, for Canada-centred themes. With no legal body, the
   grantee is not one open item among seven: it holds back fifteen
   of the thirty-nine candidates (§4).
2. **One person.** Michel is lead, administrator, deputy, first
   Curator and controller of the data. The agreement says plainly
   that the promise of a deputy cannot be kept in his absence. A
   funder will read that sentence.
3. **The test stretch.** The concept is being tried now on a short
   stretch Michel named on October 9 (kept unnamed here, since
   either end may be a host's place). A partner will ask what the
   test is, who is in it and what it records. The documents do not
   yet say; the prototype brief at Gate D must. *A question for
   Michel: does the test enter anyone's place on Plai? If so, Gate
   3's condition — backups with an off-host copy, not yet built —
   applies to it.*
4. **What is counted.** Checklist item S-7 (what Gate 4 records) is
   open; two scientific candidates and two parks turn on it, as
   does the funder's test. Until it is defined there is nothing to
   give a research partner as a baseline.
5. **No line for a study.** The budget has none. A scientific
   partner either brings its own money or the project adds a line;
   of the research funding lines found, only the DFG's special
   programme for Ukraine is open now, and it needs a Germany-based
   applicant [S40].

**What the search found that changes the picture.**

- **A "plai" route already exists in Verkhovyna.** The hromada's
  own site (its address dated September 8, 2026) presents a journey
  along the plai of the Hutsul capital — some 17 km, 12 stops with
  QR codes, free, led by the NGO Alliance of Development with the
  council and three local organisations, funded by the Ukrainian
  Cultural Foundation's community-trails line and a corporate
  community fund [S20]. Alliance of Development is one of Michel's
  two candidates. This is the closest thing to the project already
  on the ground: a partner, and also the overlap a village would
  notice first.
- **Neither the Carpathian Convention nor the Carpathian Eight has
  a fund or grant instrument.** The C8 summit announced no fund
  [S42]; no grant instrument of the Convention was found [S38].
  Both are for recognition and vocabulary. A live project under the
  Convention, in Germany's advisory programme — biodiversity,
  pastoralism and cultural heritage through education, December
  2024 to May 2027, with Ukraine among its countries [S43] — is the
  nearest door; its Ukrainian pilot areas are unverified.
- **The grantee has a realistic shape.** Änderwerk (Berlin) hosts
  projects without their own legal body and receives grants on
  their behalf; Open Collective Europe does the same at a published
  fee, with a public ledger; and a Ukrainian NGO can be the grantee
  under a written agreement that carries the partner rules. No
  Ukrainian fiscal-sponsorship service was found.
- **The national parks hold much of the ground, and one of them
  speaks the project's language.** An undated partner-search
  profile for Verkhovynskyi National Park lists preserving meadow
  ecosystems by introducing horse grazing as an activity [S26] —
  the clearest public body found that treats grazing as how meadows
  are kept. The Carpathian park is asked to approve marking in its
  territory [S21] and sells trail tickets through a booking
  platform [S24].
- **The destination bodies want what the project refuses.** The
  oblast tourism office's normal programme is a map, partner logos
  and catalogues, and it reports certification [S30]; the rural
  green tourism union runs a quality mark with categories and a
  logo [S31]. Both are on the list so Michel can strike them
  knowingly (I-11, I-12).
- **Names have changed.** Precarpathian National University is the
  Carpathian National University since August 15, 2025 [S32]; the
  Kosiv college is listed as the Kosiv State Institute of
  Decorative Art [S16]. The documents should use the new names.

## 2. Key to the table

**Turns on** — the first open item a candidate's fit depends on:
**G** the grantee (Michel, a fiscal host, an NGO, or the core body);
**P** whether a partner keeps Part I in full, no calendar and no
ratings included (S-19); **H** the hromada-council step (S-14);
**R** the research partner's standing and a consent protocol for
hosts' data; **W** what Gate 4 records (S-7); **RO** whether Romania
is in scope; **N** what Michel will not take.

**Flag** — ⚑ a risk the stress tests name for this kind of partner
(a logo on a tourism map, a booking layer, a council or association
speaking for a place, a study that would locate a host); ✕ likely
to ask for something Michel would refuse in advance.
