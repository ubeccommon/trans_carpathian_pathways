# Trans-Carpathian Pathways

*Co-Creator Agreement — what we promise each other while we build the pathway together*

October 2026 · UBEC / Carpathian Hub Initiative · Draft v0.1

## Why an agreement

Trans-Carpathian Pathways is not a service that someone runs and others use. It is made by the people in it: hosts who open their door, stewards and Curators who hold the path, travelers who walk it, and people who write its code, translate it, partner with it and fund it. A user agreement says what a service owes its users. This agreement says what each of us owes the others, and what each of us can count on.

It is written in plain words on purpose. Part I is common ground for everyone. Part II is one short part for each role. Part III says how to join. A person who holds more than one role — a host who is also a steward, say — keeps both parts.

Where something is not yet decided, this text says so, in the list "Open before signing" at the end.

## Part I — Common ground

### 1. The project and who answers for it

Trans-Carpathian Pathways is a living network of hosts, a pathway that grows from where they are, and the people who hold each stretch of it, across the Carpathian arc. Its first corridor runs through the Hutsul highlands of western Ukraine, from Vorokhta through Verkhovyna to Kosiv.

It is initiated by UBEC, the Ubuntu Bioregional Economic Commons, and led by Michel Garand. It runs on Plai, the Carpathian map of UBEC Mapservice, on UBEC's own server in Germany.

The person who answers for the project, and for the data held on Plai, is Michel Garand, reached at **stewardship@ubec.network**. *(Open A-1: whether this becomes a named legal body.)*

### 2. The roles

| Role | Who | On Plai |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Host | Someone who offers a bed, a meal, guidance or a workshop from where they live | An entrant's account, or entered by a Curator or steward with their agreement |
| Pathway steward | Someone who cares for a stretch of the path, its hosts and its places | A steward's account: enters places, never publishes |
| Curator | The memory of the path: publishes what is entered and suggests journeys to travelers | A curator's account, usually also an entrant's |
| Traveler | Anyone who walks a stretch, for an afternoon or a month | No account |
| Contributor | A developer, mapper, translator, writer or designer | Works in the project's open repositories |
| Partner initiative | A group that opens a corridor elsewhere on the arc | By a written partnership note |
| Funder | A person or foundation that supports the work | By a written funding note |
| Administrator | Whoever runs Plai and keeps its data: today Michel Garand for UBEC | The service itself |

### 3. What we all hold to

These principles decide every choice in the project. By joining, each of us agrees to hold to them.

- **Honest offer.** We offer only what we actually have, and describe it as it is.
- **No performance.** The encounter is real or it is nothing. Nobody stages tradition for visitors.
- **Organic growth.** The network grows from the inside, host by host, not from a plan drawn above it.
- **The Curator as memory.** People hold what no tool can. A tool proposes; a person decides.
- **The traveler arrives.** A journey is undergone, not consumed.
- **Off the beaten path.** Where people live, not where tourists are sent.
- **A pathway of relationships.** Person to person, person to land.
- **Data stays with the people.** Each person decides what is shown about them, can take it back, and can take it with them.
- **No calendar.** Availability beyond the season is a conversation with the Curator, never a booking grid.
- **No ratings.** Hosts are not scored, ranked or reviewed in public.
- **Safety first.** In wartime and in the mountains, safety comes before any journey, and the Curator holds what keeps people safe.

### 4. How decisions are made

- **During the pilot (Phase 1)**, the project lead decides, gate by gate, after hearing those concerned. A gate opens when its work is done, not on a date.
- **Every decision is written down**, with its date and who made it, in the project's open record (the repository `ubeccommon/trans_carpathian_pathways`). Anyone may read it.
- **Anyone may propose.** A proposal sent to the stewardship address, or raised with a Curator, is answered.
- **What is yours stays your decision.** Your own place, what is shown about you, your terms, and whether you receive a traveler are decided by you alone.
- **Before Phase 2 opens**, the hosts, stewards and Curators of the first corridor are asked how the project should be governed from there, and this agreement is changed to say so.

### 5. Your data

**What is kept.** For each place on Plai: its point on the map, its name, type, category and offering; for a host, the offers, capacities, forms of payment, months open, languages and how to reach it; a contact name, email and mobile; one photo if you add one; your consent ticks and the time you gave them; who entered it, when it was made and last changed, and its state. For each account: a user name, an email and a password. For a traveler: only what the Curator needs for the journey — a name, a way to reach you, the dates and the size of your party — kept by the Curator, not on Plai.

**Where.** On UBEC's own server in Germany, under EU law, in a database that holds this project's data and no other's.

**Why.** To put the network on the map, to let the Curator suggest journeys, and to let travelers find hosts. For nothing else. The basis is your consent, which you give field by field and can take back.

**Who sees what.**

| What | Who sees it |
| --- | --- |
| A draft | You alone |
| A place sent to the Curator | You, the Curators, the administrator |
| A published place | Everyone: its point, its description and its hosting details |
| A contact name, email, mobile or photo | The public only where you ticked that it may be shown **and** a Curator chose to show it |
| A withdrawn place | You and the administrator; never the public, never an export |
| Safety knowledge, amounts, travelers' details | The Curator, and the steward of that stretch where needed |

**What you can always do.** Change any field. Untick a consent and that detail leaves the public map at once. Withdraw a place and it leaves every map and every export. Ask for a copy of everything held about you. Ask for your places and your account to be deleted: write to the stewardship address, and it is done within 30 days. You may also complain to a data protection authority.

**What the project never does.** Sell or lend your data. Show advertising. Track what visitors to the map do. Keep access logs longer than abuse control needs *(short logs are a principle of the service not yet put in place)*. Hand your data to anyone — an authority included — unless the law compels it, and then tell you, unless the law forbids that.

**Backups.** Plai is backed up, encrypted, with a copy kept off the server, so that a fault does not lose your place. A deleted place leaves the backups as they are replaced. *(The off-site copy is a condition of the first real account and is not yet in place.)*

**If something goes wrong.** If your data is exposed, or may have been, you are told promptly, with what happened and what is being done.

**What the service cannot do yet.** It sends no email, so a forgotten password is reset by the administrator on request. A session lasts one hour; save before you step away. These are recorded and may change.

### 7. Safety, in wartime and in the mountains

- **A point is shown where it is placed.** A published place is shown exactly where its host, or the Curator or steward with the host's agreement, put it on the map. For a home, in wartime, with a name beside it, that point is an address. A host may instead place their point at a nearby meeting place — a crossroads, a chapel, a shop — and tell travelers the rest in person.
- **Safety knowledge stays with the Curator.** The nearest shelter during air-raid alerts, border-zone rules near the Romanian border, and the current state of each stage are held by the Curator, never published, and shared with a traveler only for their own journey.
- **Alerts come first.** During an air-raid alert everyone follows the official warning and the host's shelter. A journey pauses; nobody is asked to walk on.
- **The border zone.** A stage that needs a permit says so before anyone walks it.
- **Anyone may stop.** A host may refuse or end a stay. A traveler may leave a journey. A Curator may hold a stage or decline to suggest one. When safety is at stake, no reason is owed.
- **Confidential means confidential.** What a steward or Curator learns about hosts, their homes, their terms, their travelers and the path's safety is used only for the pathway, never shared beyond it, and handed back on leaving. This lasts after they leave.

### 8. Money

- **Hosts set their own terms.** For each offer a host chooses among a fixed contribution, a suggested contribution, an exchange of work, and ubec-pay or a community currency — or names none and lets the Curator give their terms. "ubec-pay" names a form of payment a host accepts; it does not mean the project runs a payment system.
- **No figure on the map.** The form of payment is public; the amount stays with the Curator.
- **Payment passes directly from traveler to host.** The project, its Curators and its stewards do not collect payment for stays, meals, guidance or workshops, and take no share of it. *(Open A-3.)*
- **No payment for sending travelers.** A Curator or steward takes no gift, fee or favour from a host for suggesting them.
- **Each answers for their own.** Each host and each paid co-creator handles their own taxes, registrations and other duties under the law where they live.
- **Funders' money is accounted for openly.** What came in, from whom (unless a funder asks not to be named) and what it was spent on is reported at each gate.
- **Paid roles are written down.** A paid Curator, steward or contributor has a separate written arrangement saying who pays, how much, for what, and for how long.

### 9. How we treat each other

- With respect: for each other's homes, customs, faith, language, rest and privacy.
- Without discrimination of any kind.
- With care for children: no child is photographed, published or left alone with a traveler without a parent's agreement.
- With honesty about who we are, what we offer and what we can do.
- Without harassment, pressure or abuse, in person or online.

Anyone who is harmed, or who sees harm, tells the Curator or writes to the stewardship address, and is heard in confidence. A Curator may hold a host's place or decline to suggest a traveler. The administrator may close an account for serious harm, after hearing the person concerned unless safety requires acting first.

### 10. What the project is not, and what nobody promises

Trans-Carpathian Pathways is not a tour operator, a travel agent or a booking service. It does not sell journeys, inspect homes, or certify anyone's skills. A Curator's route is a suggestion made in good faith by someone who knows the path; walking it is each traveler's own choice and responsibility. Nobody is promised a number of travelers, an income, or that every host will be free.

Plai is offered as it is, with care and without a guarantee that it will always be available.

Each of us answers for our own actions. Nothing in this agreement limits anyone's responsibility where the law does not allow it to be limited.

### 11. Leaving, and if the project ends

- **Anyone may leave at any time**, by telling the Curator or writing to the stewardship address. Your places can be withdrawn, deleted or handed back to you (§5).
- **What lasts after leaving:** confidentiality (§7) and the licences on code and documents already contributed (§6).
- **If the project or Plai must stop**, everyone with an account and every signed co-creator is told at least three months ahead where that is possible, each is offered a copy of their own data, and the rest is then deleted.
- **If the lead cannot be reached**, a named deputy keeps the service and its promises running. *(Open A-4.)*

### 12. When we disagree

Talk first, directly and in person where you can. If that does not settle it, bring it to the Curator of that stretch; then to the project lead; then to a mediator both sides accept. *(Open A-5: the law that governs this agreement.)*

### 13. Changing this agreement

This agreement has a version number and a dated record of every change. Every change is announced to everyone with an account and every signed co-creator at least 30 days before it applies. A change in how your data is used needs your consent again. If you do not accept a change, you may leave (§11).

## Part II — Role by role

### Host

**You agree to:**

- Offer only what you have, and keep your place on the map true: tell the Curator when you are away, when your capacity changes, or when an offer ends.
- Choose where your point is placed, knowing that it is shown exactly there once published (§7).
- Receive travelers safely: know your nearest shelter, and tell the Curator of anything a traveler must know before coming.
- Set your own terms, receive payment directly, and answer for your own duties under the law (§8).

**You can count on:**

- Your place, your words and your photo staying yours (§6); nothing public until a Curator publishes it, and no contact detail public without your tick.
- Withdrawing or changing your place at any time, and refusing any traveler without giving a reason.
- The Curator speaking with every traveler before suggesting you, and telling you who is coming, when, and how many.
- No commission, no ratings, no calendar.
- Help to enter your place: the Curator beside you, or a Curator or steward entering it for you with your agreement. A place entered for you is that person's entry under their name; they withdraw or change it for you whenever you ask.

### Pathway steward

**You agree to:**

- Care for your stretch: walk it, know its hosts and places, and tell the Curator of changes to paths, hosts and safety.
- Enter a place for a host only with their agreement, written on the sign-on sheet (Part III) and kept with the Curator; and withdraw or change it whenever they ask.
- Add places along the way — a viewpoint, a spring — truthfully, and never a private home that its people have not agreed to.
- Keep confidential what you learn (§7), and hand it back if you leave.
- Leave publishing to the Curator.

**You can count on:**

- An account, the manual, and the Curator's support.
- Being named as steward of your stretch, if you wish.
- A say in how the corridor is governed before Phase 2 (§4).

### Curator

**You agree to:**

- Read every place before publishing it: publish only offers that are honest and specific, and check that the point is where its entrant meant it.
- Before a host's home goes public, ask the host once more whether that point is where they want to be found.
- Never change an entrant's answers. Publish, show what the entrant allowed, or hold — nothing more.
- Listen to each traveler — their time, company and wish — and suggest a journey, never prescribe or sell one. Suggest only hosts who are open, with room for the party, and tell those hosts in advance.
- Hold the path's safety knowledge (§7), brief each traveler before they come, and hold a stage when it is not safe.
- Keep confidential everything you learn of hosts, amounts and travelers, use it only for the pathway, and delete a traveler's details once their journey is over. *(Open A-6: how long.)*
- Keep the signed sheets for places entered on a host's behalf.
- Take nothing from a host for sending travelers (§8).
- Hand over everything — sheets, knowledge, accounts — to the next Curator if you leave.

**You can count on:**

- A recognised role as the keeper of your stretch, and, where funding allows, a paid one under a written arrangement (§8).
- The administrator's support, and the stewards of your stretch beside you.
- Final word on what is published and what is suggested, within this agreement.

### Traveler

A traveler has no account. Before the Curator suggests a journey, a traveler reads this part and says that they accept it.

**You agree to:**

- Come as a guest, not a customer: respect the home, the customs, the rest and the rules of each host.
- Be honest about who is coming, your fitness, your experience in the mountains, and your dates.
- Pay each host directly, as you and they agreed.
- Read your own government's travel advice, understand that Ukraine is under martial law, and carry insurance that covers you there where you can obtain it.
- Follow air-raid alerts, the host's shelter, the Curator's word on safety, and any border-zone rule.
- Photograph people and homes only with their agreement, and never publish where a host lives.
- Leave paths, pastures and springs as you found them.

**You can count on:**

- An honest briefing on safety, conditions and what to expect, before you come.
- A journey suggested by someone who knows the path and its people.
- Your details held by the Curator only for your journey, and deleted after it.
- Leaving a journey at any time; telling the Curator when you do.

### Contributor — developer, mapper, translator, writer, designer

**You agree to:**

- Work in the open, in the project's repositories, under the project's licences (§6): what you contribute carries the same licence the project gives it. You confirm that you have the right to contribute it.
- Keep secrets out of the repositories, and real hosts' data out of tests, issues and screenshots: test with invented places.
- Follow the project's working rules: gate by gate, decisions written down, every document versioned. Changes to what a Plai point carries are made in `ubeccommon/ubec_mapservice`, under that project's own rules.
- As a translator, keep to the meaning; Ukrainian first, then the arc's other languages.
- As a mapper, remember that OpenStreetMap is a public database under its own terms: never add a host's home, or mark a place as hosting, in OpenStreetMap without that host's agreement.

**You can count on:**

- Credit for your work in the record of changes, and your copyright kept.
- A reply to every proposal, and a review of your work before it is used.

### Partner initiative

**You agree to:**

- Open a corridor in your country under this agreement, or under your own version of it that keeps Part I's commitments on data, consent, safety and money.
- Have your own hosts, stewards and Curators join under it, in your language.
- Agree in a short written note what each side brings, how the corridor's data is held, and how each names the other.

**You can count on:**

- The project's tools, documents and experience, under their licences.
- Your corridor's data kept under the same consent as every other, and handed to you in full if you take your corridor elsewhere — your entrants agreeing.
- Being named as partner wherever the corridor is shown.

### Funder

**You agree to:**

- Support the work as the proposal sets out, or as agreed in a written funding note.
- Leave to hosts and Curators what is published and what journeys are suggested: funding gives no say over either, and no access to hosts' or travelers' data.

**You can count on:**

- An open account at each gate of what came in and what it was spent on.
- Prompt word if the pilot stops or changes course, and unspent funds returned or redirected as agreed.
- Being named as a supporter, or not, as you wish.

### Administrator

The administrator — today Michel Garand, for UBEC — runs Plai and holds its data on everyone's behalf.

**The administrator agrees to:**

- Keep all data in the EU, on UBEC's own server, apart from every other project's data.
- Keep encrypted backups with a copy off the server, test that they restore, and keep the key where the deputy can reach it if needed. *(Open A-4.)*
- Make, change and close accounts, and reset passwords, on request.
- Delete places and accounts within 30 days of a request, and hand anyone a copy of their own data on request.
- Test, after every change to what the public can see, that nothing leaks beyond what was consented and chosen.
- Look at entries only as far as running the service needs.
- Keep every promise in §5, and tell those affected promptly of any breach.
- Announce changes and any ending as §11 and §13 say.

**The administrator can count on:**

- Co-creators reporting faults and harm promptly, and keeping their own passwords to themselves.

## Open before signing

These wait on Michel's word. Each is set out, with what bears on it, in the project review v0.1.

- **A-1 — Who answers for the project.** Michel Garand personally, as the text says now; an existing UBEC body; or a body to be formed.
- **A-2 — Rights in what is entered.** As drafted: the entrant keeps every right, and allows UBEC to store, show, back up and hand back, until they withdraw. Or an open licence, which could not be revoked once a place is published.
- **A-3 — How payment passes** (the project's open item OI-P13, second half). As drafted: directly from traveler to host, the project holding no money. Or through the Curator or a community payment system.
- **A-4 — Continuity.** Who is the deputy administrator, and who holds the second copy of the backup key.
- **A-5 — Governing law** and the last step of a dispute.
- **A-6 — How long things are kept:** a withdrawn place that is never sent again; a traveler's details after their journey.
- **A-7 — How each role signs.** As drafted: hosts on paper with the Curator, in Ukrainian.

Before the first real account, someone who knows German data protection law reads §5; before the first traveler, someone who knows Ukrainian tourism law reads §8 and §10.
