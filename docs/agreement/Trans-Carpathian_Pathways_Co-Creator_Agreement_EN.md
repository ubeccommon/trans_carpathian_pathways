# Trans-Carpathian Pathways

*Co-Creator Agreement — what we promise each other while we build the pathway together*

October 2026 · UBEC / Carpathian Hub Initiative · Draft v0.7

## Why an agreement

Trans-Carpathian Pathways is not a service that someone runs and others use. It is made by the people in it: hosts who open their door, stewards and Curators who hold the path, pilgrims who walk it, and people who write its code, translate it, partner with it and fund it. A user agreement says what a service owes its users. This agreement says what each of us owes the others, and what each of us can count on.

It is written in plain words on purpose. Part I is common ground for everyone. Part II is one short part for each role. Part III says how to join. A person who holds more than one role — a host who is also a steward, say — keeps both parts.

Where something is not yet decided, this text says so, in the list "Open before signing" at the end. Where a decision was Michel Garand's, the text gives its date.

## Part I — Common ground

### 1. The project and who answers for it

Trans-Carpathian Pathways is a living network of hosts, a pathway that grows from where they are, and the people who hold each stretch of it, across the Carpathian arc. Its first corridor runs through the Hutsul highlands of western Ukraine, from Vorokhta through Verkhovyna to Kosiv.

Initiated by UBEC (Ubuntu Bioregional Economic Commons), led by Michel Garand, and Zhyva Khata, led by Vasyl Yuskiv. It runs on Plai, the Carpathian map of UBEC Mapservice, on UBEC's own server in Germany.

The person who answers for the project, and for the data held on Plai, is Michel Garand, reached at **stewardship@ubec.network** — personally, until a core body is formed, which is planned for the near future. That body will hold the Plai instance, the project's name and marks, and the role of controller of the data, and this agreement will then name it. *(Michel's word, October 9, 2026; A-1.)*

### 2. The roles

| Role | Who | On Plai |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Host | Someone who offers a bed, a meal, guidance or a workshop from where they live | An entrant's account, or entered by a Curator or steward with their agreement |
| Pathway steward | Someone who cares for a stretch of the path, its hosts and its places | A steward's account: enters places, never publishes |
| Curator | The memory of the path: publishes what is entered and suggests journeys to pilgrims | A curator's account, usually also an entrant's |
| Pilgrim of the Plai | Anyone who walks a stretch of the plai, the old path through the high pastures, for an afternoon or a month, and arrives as a guest | No account |
| Contributor | A developer, mapper, translator, writer or designer | Works in the project's repositories |
| Partner initiative | A group that opens a corridor elsewhere on the arc | By a written partnership note |
| Funder | A person or foundation that supports the work | By a written funding note |
| Administrator | Whoever runs Plai and keeps its data: today Michel Garand for UBEC | The service itself |

### 3. What we all hold to

These principles decide every choice in the project. By joining, each of us agrees to hold to them.

- **Honest offer.** We offer only what we actually have, and describe it as it is.
- **No performance.** The encounter is real or it is nothing. Nobody stages tradition for visitors.
- **Organic growth.** The network grows from the inside, host by host, not from a plan drawn above it.
- **The Curator as memory.** People hold what no tool can. A tool proposes; a person decides.
- **The pilgrim arrives.** A journey is undergone, not consumed.
- **Off the beaten path.** Where people live, not where tourists are sent.
- **A pathway of relationships.** Person to person, person to land.
- **No one speaks for a community.** A host speaks for their own place, a Curator for what they publish, the project for itself.
- **Not wilderness.** People and land are one and the same; what needs protecting is the relationship.
- **Data stays with the people.** Each person decides what is shown about them, can take it back, and can take it with them.
- **No calendar.** Availability beyond the season is a conversation with the Curator, never a booking grid.
- **No ratings.** Hosts are not scored, ranked or reviewed in public.
- **Safety first.** In wartime and in the mountains, safety comes before any journey, and the Curator holds what keeps people safe.
- **A place is shown where its host puts it.** The host places the point; the Curator publishes or holds the entry.

### 4. How decisions are made

- **During the pilot (Phase 1)**, the project lead decides, gate by gate, after hearing those concerned. A gate opens when its work is done, not on a date.
- **Every decision is written down**, with its date and who made it, in the project's record. The documents published for review are on the project's site; the rest of the record is shared with anyone who takes part.
- **Anyone may propose.** A proposal sent to the stewardship address, or raised with a Curator, is answered.
- **What is yours stays your decision.** Your own place, what is shown about you, your terms, and whether you receive a pilgrim are decided by you alone.
- **Before Phase 2 opens**, the co-creators of the first corridor — its hosts, stewards, Curators and the others who build it — decide how the project is governed from there, and this agreement is changed to say what they decided. The first corridor receives a partnership note of its own (Part II, partner initiative), with the same right to leave with its data, its map and its name as any partner. *(Michel's word, October 9, 2026.)*

### 5. Your data

**What is kept.** For each place on Plai: its point on the map, its name, type, category and offering; for a host, the offers, capacities, forms of payment, months open, languages and how to reach it; a contact name, email and mobile; one photo if you add one; your consent ticks and the time you gave them; who entered it, when it was made and last changed, and its state. For each account: a user name, an email and a password. For a pilgrim: only what the Curator needs for the journey — a name, a way to reach you, the dates and the size of your party — kept by the Curator, not on Plai. For an incident (§9): a minimal record — what happened, when, who reported it, what was done — kept by the Curator, and the deputy once named, not on Plai.

**Where.** On UBEC's own server in Germany, under EU law, in Plai's own database, apart from every other project's data.

**Why.** To put the network on the map, to let the Curator suggest journeys, and to let pilgrims find hosts. For nothing else. A place is used in a submission to a public body — a corridor put before a ministry, a council or the Carpathian Eight — only with a separate consent from its host, which can be withdrawn at any time; a host may stay on the map and decline it. A corridor is shown as a broad band, never as parcels, and never with a count of consents set against the people who live inside it. *(Michel's word, October 9, 2026, S-14.)* The basis is your consent, which you give field by field and can take back.

**Who sees what.**

| What | Who sees it |
| --- | --- |
| A draft | You alone |
| A place sent to the Curator | You, the Curators, the administrator |
| A published place | Everyone: its point, its description and its hosting details |
| A contact name, email, mobile or photo | The public only where you ticked that it may be shown **and** a Curator chose to show it |
| A withdrawn place | You and the administrator; never the public, never an export |
| Safety knowledge, amounts, pilgrims' details, the incident record | The Curator, and the steward of that stretch where needed — never Plai, which holds no confidential information (§7) |

**What you can always do.** Change any field. Untick a consent and that detail leaves the public map at once. Withdraw a place and it leaves every map and every export. Once published, though, a point may have been copied by others: withdrawal removes it from Plai, not from copies. Ask for a copy of everything held about you. Ask for your places and your account to be deleted: write to the stewardship address, and it is done within 30 days. You may also complain to a data protection authority.

**What the project never does.** Sell or lend your data. Show advertising. Track what visitors to the map do. Keep access logs longer than abuse control needs *(the map's public face keeps its access logs for seven days, since October 9, 2026)*. Hand your data to anyone — an authority included — unless the law compels it, and then tell you, unless the law forbids that.

**Backups.** Before the first real account, Plai's backups are encrypted, copied off the server and tested to restore, so that a fault does not lose your place. A deleted place leaves the backups as they are replaced.

**If something goes wrong.** If your data is exposed, or may have been, you are told promptly, with what happened and what is being done.

**What the service cannot do yet.** It sends no email, so a forgotten password is reset by the administrator, only after the request is confirmed with you in person or through your Curator. Nobody from the project will ever ask you for your password. A session lasts one hour; save before you step away. These are recorded and may change.

### 7. Safety, in wartime and in the mountains

- **A point is shown where it is placed.** A published place is shown exactly where its host, or the Curator or steward with the host's agreement, put it on the map. For a home, in wartime, with a name beside it, that point is an address. A host may instead place their point at a nearby meeting place — a crossroads, a chapel, a shop — and must then say so plainly in the place's own information, so that a pilgrim knows the point is not the house; the rest is told in person. Nobody proposes this to a host; it is the host's own choice. *(Michel's word, October 9, 2026.)*
- **Plai holds no confidential information.** The rules of the border zone near the Romanian border are public law: the Curator gives them in writing to every pilgrim who comes through the Curator, and the project publishes them for anyone who comes directly (Part II). The nearest shelter during air-raid alerts, the current state of each stage and anything learned about a host's safety are held by the Curator, off the platform, never published, and shared with a pilgrim only for their own journey. *(Michel's word, October 9, 2026.)*
- **Alerts come first.** During an air-raid alert everyone follows the official warning and goes to the nearest shelter the host knows of. A journey pauses; nobody is asked to walk on.
- **The border zone.** No stage inside the five-kilometre border strip is suggested to anyone until the Curator holds, in writing from the border detachment, how the permit is obtained — for a foreigner too — and has obtained one once. The Curator keeps a stage register, one sheet per stage: whether the stage lies in the strip, what permit it needs and how, its current state, and when it was last checked. *(Michel's word, October 9, 2026.)*
- **Anyone may stop.** A host may refuse or end a stay. A pilgrim may leave a journey. A Curator may hold a stage or decline to suggest one. When safety is at stake, no reason is owed.
- **Confidential means confidential.** What a steward or Curator learns about hosts, their homes, their terms, their pilgrims and the path's safety is used only for the pathway, never shared beyond it, and handed back on leaving. This lasts after they leave.

### 8. Money

- **Hosts set their own terms.** For each offer a host chooses among a fixed contribution, a suggested contribution, an exchange of work, and ubec-pay or a community currency — or names none and lets the Curator give their terms. "ubec-pay" names a form of payment a host accepts; it does not mean the project runs a payment system.
- **ubec-pay and a community currency.** A host who ticks this form accepts settlement in a community currency, or in a mutual-credit account that UBEC keeps for the people in its commons, instead of in money. It is a form a host may accept; the project runs no payment system and holds no money. What one unit is worth and where it can be spent is for the community that issues it, and is told to a pilgrim before they rely on it. The hryvnia remains the only legal tender, and each party's duties under the law — tax on income received in kind among them — stay their own. *(Kept on Michel's word, October 9, 2026; the definition is proposed wording, to be confirmed before signing.)*
- **A contribution is not a price.** A price buys a service, and makes the one who sets it a supplier and the one who pays it a customer. A contribution is what a host names to cover what receiving a guest costs them and to acknowledge their work: set by the host and not by a market, not bargained over, and paid by the pilgrim as a guest. Nothing more is owed for it and nothing is bought by it — the welcome, the table and the knowledge shared are not for sale, and the host may still refuse, end a stay, or give more than was named. A fixed contribution is a sum the host asks for; a suggested one is a sum the host names and the pilgrim may exceed or, by agreement, fall short of. *(Proposed wording, to be confirmed before signing.)*
- **No figure on the map.** The form of payment is shown on the map; the amount is told to the Curator and never shown, and the Curator never takes or holds the money.
- **Payment passes directly from pilgrim to host.** The project, its Curators and its stewards do not collect payment for stays, meals, guidance or workshops, and take no share of it. *(Michel's word, October 9, 2026; A-3 closed.)*
- **No payment for sending pilgrims.** A Curator or steward takes no gift, fee or favour from a host for suggesting them.
- **Each answers for their own.** Each host and each paid co-creator handles their own taxes, registrations and other duties under the law where they live.
- **Funders' money is accounted for openly.** What came in, from whom (unless a funder asks not to be named) and what it was spent on is reported at each gate.
- **Paid roles are written down.** Curators and stewards are paid roles. Who pays, how much, for what and for how long, and what happens when a grant ends, is decided once funding or a financing plan is secured, and written in a separate arrangement for each paid Curator, steward or contributor. *(Michel's word, October 9, 2026.)*

### 9. How we treat each other

- With respect: for each other's homes, customs, faith, language, rest and privacy.
- Without discrimination of any kind.
- With care for children: no child is photographed or published without a parent's agreement, and no pilgrim is left alone with a child.
- With honesty about who we are, what we offer and what we can do.
- Without harassment, pressure or abuse, in person or online.

Anyone who is harmed, or who sees harm, tells the Curator or writes to the stewardship address, and is heard in confidence. A Curator may hold a host's place or decline to suggest a pilgrim; a hold on a host's place that lasts beyond 30 days carries a written reason to the host. The Curator, and the deputy once named, keep a minimal record of each incident — what happened, when, who reported it, what was done — off Plai, for the time A-6 sets, never public. The administrator may close an account for serious harm, after hearing the person concerned unless safety requires acting first.

### 10. What the project is not, and what nobody promises

Trans-Carpathian Pathways is not a tour operator, a travel agent or a booking service. It does not sell journeys, inspect homes, or certify anyone's skills. A Curator's route is a suggestion made in good faith by someone who knows the path; walking it is each pilgrim's own choice and responsibility. Nobody is promised a number of pilgrims, an income, or that every host will be free.

Plai is offered as it is, with care and without a guarantee that it will always be available.

Each of us answers for our own actions. Nothing in this agreement limits anyone's responsibility where the law does not allow it to be limited.

### 11. Leaving, and if the project ends

- **Anyone may leave at any time**, by telling the Curator or writing to the stewardship address. Your places can be withdrawn, deleted or handed back to you (§5).
- **What lasts after leaving:** confidentiality (§7) and the licences on code and documents already contributed (§6).
- **If the project or Plai must stop**, everyone with an account and every signed co-creator is told at least three months ahead where that is possible, each is offered a copy of their own data, and the rest is then deleted.
- **If the lead cannot be reached**, a named deputy keeps the service and its promises running. No deputy is named yet; the core body, once formed, names one. Until then the project's promises depend on one person being reachable, and this agreement says so plainly. *(Michel's word, October 9, 2026; A-4 passes to the body.)*

### 12. When we disagree

Talk first, directly and in person where you can. If that does not settle it, bring it to the Curator of that stretch; then to the project lead; then to a mediator both sides accept. *(Open A-5: the law that governs this agreement.)*

### 13. Changing this agreement

This agreement has a version number and a dated record of every change. Every change is announced to everyone with an account and every signed co-creator at least 30 days before it applies. A change in how your data is used needs your consent again. If you do not accept a change, you may leave (§11).

## Part II — Role by role

### Host

**You agree to:**

- Offer only what you have, and keep your place on the map true: tell the Curator when you are away, when your capacity changes, or when an offer ends.
- Choose where your point is placed, knowing that it is shown exactly there once published (§7); if you place it at a meeting place rather than your house, say so in your place's information.
- Receive pilgrims safely: know your nearest shelter, and tell the Curator of anything a pilgrim must know before coming.
- Set your own terms, receive payment directly, and answer for your own duties under the law (§8).

**You can count on:**

- Your place, your words and your photo staying yours (§6); nothing public until a Curator publishes it, and no contact detail public without your tick.
- Withdrawing or changing your place at any time, and refusing any pilgrim without giving a reason.
- For pilgrims who come through the Curator: the Curator speaking with them before suggesting you, and telling you who is coming, when, and how many. Pilgrims may also find your published place themselves and come directly — the Curator is not a gatekeeper between you and them — and you may refuse anyone.
- No commission, no ratings, no calendar.
- Nobody from the project asking you for money, a share, a password, or your passport or a copy of it. The project writes only from stewardship@ubec.network; anyone else who claims to speak for it is not the project.
- Help to enter your place: the Curator beside you, or a Curator or steward entering it for you with your agreement. A place entered for you is that person's entry under their name; they withdraw or change it for you whenever you ask.

### Pathway steward

**You agree to:**

- Care for your stretch: walk it, know its hosts and places, and tell the Curator of changes to paths, hosts and safety.
- Enter a place for a host only with their agreement, written on the sign-on sheet (Part III) and kept with the Curator; and withdraw or change it whenever they ask.
- Add places along the way — a viewpoint, a spring — truthfully, and never a private home that its people have not agreed to.
- Keep confidential what you learn (§7), and hand it back if you leave.
- Leave publishing to the Curator.

**You can count on:**

- An account, the manual, and the Curator's support.
- Being named as steward of your stretch, if you wish.
- A say in how the corridor is governed before Phase 2 (§4).

### Curator

**You agree to:**

- Read every place before publishing it: publish only offers that are honest and specific, and check that the point is where its entrant meant it.
- Before a host's home goes public, ask the host once more whether that point is where they want to be found.
- Never change an entrant's answers. Publish, show what the entrant allowed, or hold — nothing more.
- Listen to each pilgrim — their time, company and wish — and suggest a journey, never prescribe or sell one. Suggest only hosts who are open, with room for the party, and tell those hosts in advance.
- Hold the path's safety knowledge off the platform (§7), keep the stage register, brief each pilgrim who comes through you before they come, and hold a stage when it is not safe.
- Keep an overview of the carrying capacity of each host, each stretch and the pathway as a whole, and suggest nothing beyond it.
- Keep, with the deputy once named, the minimal incident record (§9).
- Keep confidential everything you learn of hosts, amounts and pilgrims, use it only for the pathway, and delete a pilgrim's details once their journey is over. *(Open A-6: how long.)*
- Keep the signed sheets for places entered on a host's behalf.
- Take nothing from a host for sending pilgrims (§8).
- Hand over everything — sheets, knowledge, accounts — to the next Curator if you leave.

**You can count on:**

- A recognised role as the keeper of your stretch, and, where funding allows, a paid one under a written arrangement (§8).
- The administrator's support, and the stewards of your stretch beside you.
- Final word on what is published and what is suggested, within this agreement.

### Pilgrim of the Plai

*The plai is the old Carpathian path through the high pastures, and the map takes its name from it. A pilgrim of the plai comes not to a shrine but to the mountain and the people who keep it alive.*

A pilgrim has no account. A pilgrim may ask the Curator to suggest a journey, or may find a published place on Plai and go directly; the Curator is not a gatekeeper. Before the Curator suggests a journey, a pilgrim reads this part and says that they accept it; a pilgrim who goes directly is bound by it all the same. *(Michel's word, October 9, 2026.)*

**You agree to:**

- Come as a guest, not a customer: respect the home, the customs, the rest and the rules of each host.
- Be honest about who is coming, your fitness, your experience in the mountains, and your dates.
- Pay each host directly, as you and they agreed.
- Be responsible for your own preparation and travel, as every traveler abroad is: inform yourself, in your own country, about travel advisories, insurance, martial law, cash and currency, and the permits the border zone requires. The project tells you what it knows (§7) and promises no more.
- Follow air-raid alerts, the nearest shelter the host knows of, the Curator's word on safety, and any border-zone rule.
- Photograph people and homes only with their agreement, and never publish where a host lives.
- Leave paths, pastures and springs as you found them.

**You can count on:**

- An honest briefing on safety, conditions and what to expect, before you come, if you come through the Curator.
- A journey suggested by someone who knows the path and its people.
- Your details held by the Curator only for your journey, and deleted after it.
- Leaving a journey at any time; telling the Curator when you do.
- Nobody from the project — the lead, a Curator or a steward — asking you for money in advance, a deposit, a password, or your passport or a copy of it. You pay the host, as you agreed. The project writes only from stewardship@ubec.network; anyone else who claims to speak for it is not the project.

### Contributor — developer, mapper, translator, writer, designer

**You agree to:**

- Work in the open, in the project's repositories, under the project's licences (§6): what you contribute carries the same licence the project gives it. You confirm that you have the right to contribute it.
- Keep secrets out of the repositories, and real hosts' data out of tests, issues and screenshots: test with invented places.
- Follow the project's working rules: gate by gate, decisions written down, every document versioned. Changes to what a Plai point carries are made in `ubeccommon/ubec_mapservice`, under that project's own rules.
- As a translator, keep to the meaning; Ukrainian first, then the arc's other languages.
- As a mapper, remember that OpenStreetMap is a public database under its own terms: never add a host's home, or mark a place as hosting, in OpenStreetMap without that host's agreement.

**You can count on:**

- Credit for your work in the record of changes, and your copyright kept.
- A reply to every proposal, and a review of your work before it is used.

### Partner initiative

**You agree to:**

- Open a corridor in your country under this agreement, or under your own version of it that keeps Part I's commitments on data, consent, safety and money.
- Have your own hosts, stewards and Curators join under it, in your language.
- Agree in a short written note what each side brings, how the corridor's data is held, and how each names the other.

**You can count on:**

- The project's tools, documents and experience, under their licences.
- Your corridor's data kept under the same consent as every other, and handed to you in full if you take your corridor elsewhere — your entrants agreeing.
- Being named as partner wherever the corridor is shown, and in the public list of partners the project keeps from the start. *(Michel's word, October 9, 2026.)*

The first corridor is itself a partner under this part (§4).

### Funder

**You agree to:**

- Support the work as the proposal sets out, or as agreed in a written funding note.
- Leave to hosts and Curators what is published and what journeys are suggested: funding gives no say over either, and no access to hosts' or pilgrims' data.

**You can count on:**

- An open account at each gate of what came in and what it was spent on.
- Prompt word if the pilot stops or changes course, and unspent funds returned or redirected as agreed.
- Being named as a supporter, or not, as you wish.

### Administrator

The administrator — today Michel Garand, for UBEC — runs Plai and holds its data on everyone's behalf.

**The administrator agrees to:**

- Keep all data in the EU, on UBEC's own server, apart from every other project's data.
- Keep encrypted backups with a copy off the server, test that they restore, and keep the key where the deputy can reach it if needed. *(Open A-4.)*
- Make, change and close accounts, and reset passwords, on request.
- Delete places and accounts within 30 days of a request, and hand anyone a copy of their own data on request.
- Test, after every change to what the public can see, that nothing leaks beyond what was consented and chosen.
- Look at entries only as far as running the service needs.
- Keep every promise in §5, and tell those affected promptly of any breach.
- Announce changes and any ending as §11 and §13 say.

**The administrator can count on:**

- Co-creators reporting faults and harm promptly, and keeping their own passwords to themselves.

## Part III — Joining

## Open before signing, and what was settled

Michel's word of October 9, 2026 settled four of the seven items drafted on October 8; what remains is listed here, with what bears on it in the project review v0.1.

- **A-1 — Who answers for the project.** Decided: Michel Garand personally until a core body is formed, planned for the near future, which will hold the Plai instance, the marks and the controller role (§1).
- **A-2 — Rights in what is entered.** As drafted, the entrant keeps every right and allows UBEC to store, show, back up and hand back until they withdraw. Michel's answer of October 9 is read as this option; to be confirmed, since an open licence could not be revoked once a place is published.
- **A-3 — How payment passes.** Decided: directly from pilgrim to host, the project holding no money (§8).
- **A-4 — Continuity.** Passes to the core body. Today Michel Garand holds both roles and there is no second person (§11).
- **A-5 — Governing law** and the last step of a dispute. Open.
- **A-6 — How long things are kept:** a withdrawn place that is never sent again; a pilgrim's details after their journey; the incident record (§9). Open.
- **A-7 — How each role signs.** Decided: hosts on paper with the Curator, in Ukrainian (Part III).
- **The version on Plai.** Plai links the draft in force and records, with every entry, the version its maker accepted and when; a place keeps the version it was entered under.
