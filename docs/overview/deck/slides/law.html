Conditions

Designed for wartime, within the law

The border strip

Within 5 km of the state line, leaving a village needs a border-guard permit. No stage there until the permit route is held in writing.

Pilgrims from abroad

Western governments advise against travel. Each pilgrim reads their own advice; the Curator briefs those who come through the Curator.

Hosting from home

Up to 30 places without a business, if the host declares the income and keeps a guest register (Procedure 297).

Data

Held in Germany; contacts and photo shown only by the host's tick; handed to no one unless the law compels it.

Two legal reads come first: data protection before the first real account, tourism and tax law before the first pilgrim.

Not legal advice; sources in the white paper, §10 · 12

The pilot begins in Ukraine during the war, and is designed around that fact rather than despite it. The curfew in Ivano-Frankivsk oblast runs from midnight to five; during an air-raid alert a journey pauses. In Verkhovyna raion, a border-detachment order restricts routes to Pip Ivan, Pozhyzhevska and Lake Brebeneskul. Each host answers for their own tax and registrations, and may register as a sole proprietor instead; whether serving meals needs a food registration is a grey zone. A payment for a bed is income whatever it is called, which is why the project takes no money and means to sell no tourist product; whether its shape needs any licence, and which data-protection law governs it, are the first legal reads. The case for inviting pilgrims at all, solidarity, income and witness, and the case against, risk to guests and a home made visible, are both real.
