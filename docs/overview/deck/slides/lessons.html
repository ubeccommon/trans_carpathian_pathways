Sources in the white paper, §7 · 10

Via Transilvanica in Romania: about 1,400 kilometres built by one NGO with volunteers, an annual budget over 2.5 million euro, and 300 to 400 kilometres of marking to refresh every year; visitor counts drift between estimates. Via Dinarica: donor-built from 2010, still relying on a donor-funded support centre. The Czech club keeps about 43,000 kilometres marked with a volunteer assigned to each section; Slovakia renewed only 1,200 kilometres in 2025 for lack of funds. Goodwin and Santilli found only six of fifteen community-tourism projects economically sustainable, and many lodgings at about 5 per cent occupancy. The Appalachian Trail began in 1921 as a project of regional planning: the trail was built, the community camps were not, and compulsory purchase of a corridor met hard resistance. The lesson Pathways takes: keep the living part of the vision, not only the path.
